# nTop writing style

Write for a reader who must assess or use the engineering result.
State the main finding early. Explain its evidence and limits in connected prose.

## Technical mechanics

- Use active voice and simple tenses. Name the actor or tool when that affects meaning.
- Keep most sentences within 20 to 25 words and give each sentence one main idea.
- Use one term for each concept. Define an acronym at first use.
- Prefer concrete verbs and familiar words. Break long noun groups into phrases.
- State units, tolerances, operating conditions, source revision, and reference conventions where relevant.
- Label supplied, assumed, calculated, inferred, and measured values distinctly.
- Tie claims to a source, method, or test. State what remains unknown or failed.
- Use no emojis or em dashes. Avoid slogans, inflated adjectives, fragment stacks, and rhetorical reversals.

These mechanics follow the project's Simplified Technical English preference.
They do not imply that a document has received a formal ASD-STE100 compliance assessment.

For informal correspondence, allow a natural voice while preserving factual accuracy.
For reports and papers, distinguish the proposed method, its implementation, and its demonstrated validation.
Do not describe a planned skill as installed or a numerical screen as manufacturing release.

Before delivery, remove words that add no meaning. Check every strong claim, unexplained acronym, numerical value, and caption.
