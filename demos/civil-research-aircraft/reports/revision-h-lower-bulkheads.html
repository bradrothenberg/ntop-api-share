01Replace the isolated pad with a continuous side section

The small loop in Revision G was a retained side-longeron passage pad. A narrow ring connected it to the bottom frame. The new geometry restores the available lower fuselage section around that passage. Six additional conformal pockets per face divide each primary's two side sections into shorter web bays.

Both lower side sections are rebuilt and connected to the bottom frame. The passage pad is now part of the broader frame.

The pin centers, central opening, longeron routes and outboard spars remain fixed. The new section adds calculated mass. It does not establish structural strength or manufacturing release.

At waterline 100 mm, the forward side band increases from 57.3 to 176.0 mm. The aft band increases from 56.3 to 172.9 mm. These are projected contour widths. They are not solid rectangular sections: pockets leave the proposed 10 mm residual web.

Mass uses section integration at an assumed aluminum density of 2,830 kg/m3. The 6-to-12-point quadrature change is below 0.002 percent. The calculation includes pocket floors, rolling radii and face reliefs. It is not a native mass measurement or an aircraft weight optimization.

The proposed axial stock stays 76 mm. The pocket plan radius stays 12.7 mm, and the rolling floor radius stays 6.35 mm. Native samples check the pocket voids, floors and curved roots. The broad webs replace the prior narrow strip without filling the side section solid.

Analysis scope

This change does not resolve Revision G's fork transition stress failure. Its calculated peaks remain 411 MPa forward and 462 MPa aft against an unvalidated 250 MPa ceiling. No new overall aircraft or lower-frame shell solve is claimed. Buckling, joint contact, fatigue and manufacturing release remain open.
