The native two-rail sweep uses a straight axial station spine with curved center and orientation guides. Equal axial guide spacing prevents local guide folds.

The 2 mm value describes the transverse profile. Minimum wall thickness normal to the curved swept surface has not been certified.

Use Adaptive preview for close inspection of an isolated hat. Fixed-resolution GPU views can produce false ladder-like gaps. Native fields and adaptive close-ups confirm continuous walls.

The full assembly uses fixed sampling. The separate longeron and joint views use Adaptive. At aircraft scale, either mode can leave thin walls stippled; use the close-ups to inspect their shape.

02Clearances, pads, and retained contacts

Each passage follows measured native sections on five planes through the largest supported frame thickness. A filled convex envelope removes trapped frame material inside the hat cavity.

A proposed 3 mm rounded offset surrounds that envelope. Intermediate frames gain local inner pads with a proposed 8 mm land. Primary pockets keep clear of the new openings.

Three upper longerons move an additional 6 mm inward at the aft primary station. This preserves the outer bulkhead land above their passages.

The wing and tail spars, ribs, and root supports retain their saved Revision D definitions. Native point checks verify the revised frame faces at their retained wing and tail contacts.

Passage clearance permits assembly but does not provide a load-transfer connection. Skin attachments, longeron clips, fasteners, and splice fittings remain to be designed.

The STEP files use independent Open CASCADE solids built from the same perimeter, opening, and pocket-core coordinates as nTop.

  1. Extrude the blank and access opening, then add the perimeter edge breaks.
  2. Create both sets of pocket cutters from the shared planar cores.
  3. Apply the R6.35 floor fillets and subtract the cutters.
  4. Cut the new rounded hat envelopes through the blank.
  5. Write STEP, re-import it, and check solid validity and connectivity.
  6. Compare 60,000 tessellation samples per part with the native construction formula. Check the native fields separately.

These are regenerated B-rep files. The method does not directly export the implicit bodies to STEP. The comparison is finite sampling, not a global CAM tolerance certificate.

Embedded definitions are snapshots. Editing a standalone notebook requires a deliberate assembly refresh.

  1. Edit the part or its reproducible geometry recipe.
  2. Save and export the complete native definition with its input and output contracts.
  3. Refresh the corresponding assembly slot and compare the expanded output expressions.
  4. For a route or exterior section change, measure the native swept sections again and rebuild the affected frame passages.
  5. Repeat the native contact and clearance checks. Regenerate affected STEP files and views.

06Analysis status and remaining design work

The user supplied an approximate 7,500 lb aircraft weight. The earlier buckling study used assumed loads and Revision D geometry.

That study rejected uniform primary bulkhead thinning. Its local results do not establish the strength of the installed Revision E hats or their new passages.

Next structural work

Apply defined maneuver, gust, landing, and equipment loads to the revised assembly. Include skin restraint, joint stiffness, and the actual open-section geometry.

Check local plate buckling, flexural-torsional buckling, load transfer at clips, and the reduced frame lands before sizing the walls further.

Hat bend radii, manufacturing process, material temper, attachment details, forging supply, machining tolerances, and CAM verification remain open. This revision is a geometry study.
