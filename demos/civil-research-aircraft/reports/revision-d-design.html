01The structure inside the aircraft

The supplied aircraft measures approximately 9.544 m in length and 9.144 m in span in the retained source mesh. Gross weight is the user’s approximately 7,500 lb assumption.

The proposed structure uses two carry-through bulkheads, ten intermediate frames, eight longerons, and two spars per wing. Each canted tail contains two spars and five ribs.

The continuous rings route the proposed wing load paths around the fuselage opening. Pocket webs connect their inner and outer flanges. Skin and joint definitions must complete the structural load paths.

02Shoulder pockets and corrected corners

The old construction extended some rib cutters across the full ring. Those cutters nicked remote pocket boundaries and produced the hooked corner.

Each cutter now acts only on its local crossing of the ring. The revised construction also retains two small, rounded shoulder pockets per face.

03How the members fit

The primary bulkhead end faces and wing spar roots share the planes Y = ±1,280 mm. Spar caps use a proposed 58.5 mm chord width to fit inside the bulkhead edge breaks.

Each wing rib ends at the actual inside spar web faces. The contact follows both segments where a rib straddles a spar change in direction.

The ribs retain end lands after trimming. Separate cap relief provides a proposed 0.5 mm allowance without opening a gap at the web contact.

The tail checks exposed inconsistent contact at the old spar ends. Continuous webs now extend past the outer rib faces. The tail ribs use those same web planes.

04How the STEP files were made

The STEP files are independent Open CASCADE boundary-representation models. They are not direct exports of the nTop implicit bodies.

  1. Read the same outer profile, central opening, and pocket-core coordinates used by the native nTop model.
  2. Extrude the blank and central opening. Add the perimeter edge breaks.
  3. Offset each planar core to form the R12.7 pocket boundary. Extrude a cutter for each face.
  4. Apply R6.35 wall-to-floor fillets to the cutter, then subtract it from the blank.
  5. Cut the eight oblique longeron passages.
  6. Write STEP, re-import it, and check that it contains one valid solid.
  7. Compare an independent tessellation with the exact native construction formula and check the native field separately.

07Material and manufacturing proposal

7050-T7452 remains the candidate forged alloy. Aubert & Duval describes compression stress relief and the T74 balance of strength, toughness, fatigue, and corrosion resistance. Supplier typical values are not design allowables.

Primary material source: Aubert & Duval 7050 data sheet.
